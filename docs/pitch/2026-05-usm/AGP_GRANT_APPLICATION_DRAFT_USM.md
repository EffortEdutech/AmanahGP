# Grant Application Draft

**Project title:** Digitising Amanah: A Behavioural and Digital Governance Pilot for Islamic Social Finance Organizations in Malaysia  
**Applicant structure:** Amanah Governance Platform / Effort Edutech as direct applicant, with Prof. Anuar as advising member and JAKIM/MAIN as proposed grant, development, or implementation partner  
**Advising member target:** Associate Professor Dr. Mohd Anuar bin Arshad, School of Management, Universiti Sains Malaysia  
**Funding request placeholder:** RM550,000 for a 12-month pilot  
**Pilot scale placeholder:** 10-20 mosques, Islamic NGOs, waqf initiatives, or community charities  
**Prepared on:** 8 May 2026

## 1. Executive Summary

Malaysia's Islamic social finance ecosystem is large, generous, and institutionally important, but many community-level charities, mosques, and NGOs still rely on fragmented reporting, manual evidence management, and informal trust. Amanah Governance Platform (AGP) addresses this gap by providing a digital governance layer for verified charity transparency, organization-side compliance operations, evaluator review workflows, and public Amanah Index trust profiles.

AGP is designed as public-interest infrastructure rather than a commercial product. The platform will not collect payment from charity organizations. Instead, its sustainability model will rely on grant funding, corporate ESG/CSR sponsorship, and zakat/waqf ecosystem support so that charities can receive governance services without a direct financial burden.

AGP is already live with seeded/mock public data. AmanahHub's `/charities` page currently demonstrates 46 visible organisations and 37 published trust profiles, with examples across mosque/surau, waqf institution, zakat body, foundation/yayasan, cooperative, welfare association and other categories. The platform is therefore not a paper concept: it is a deployed MVP with public trust cards, scoring/tier language, and live protected login surfaces for amanahOS and AGP Console.

This project proposes a 12-month AGP-led pilot with JAKIM/MAIN as proposed grant, development, or implementation partner. Prof. Anuar will be approached as an advising member for organizational behaviour, learning, ethics, and capability-building dimensions. This approach keeps AGP as the direct grant applicant while still benefiting from expert advisory input.

This is not an exclusive route. AGP will keep all available funding routes open, including JAKIM/MAIN, JAWHAR, MDEC, Hasanah, Cradle, SC FIKRA, IERIF/INCEIF, IsDB, CSR/ESG sponsorships, state programmes, OIC-region opportunities, and university-linked research co-applications where useful.

The project is intentionally not framed as software alone. It is a digital trust and behaviour change intervention. The expected result is a tested model for helping Malaysian Islamic charities improve governance discipline, evidence readiness, donor confidence, and organizational learning.

## 2. Problem Statement

Islamic giving depends on amanah: trustworthiness, accountability, and care in managing funds intended for public benefit. Yet many grassroots organizations face practical constraints:

- governance documents and reports are stored in scattered formats;
- donor-facing transparency is inconsistent;
- administrators may lack training in reporting, evidence management, and digital compliance;
- reviewers and scholars often lack a structured workflow for clarifications and approvals;
- donors cannot easily distinguish between organizations with strong governance and those with incomplete reporting;
- oversight tends to be reactive after problems emerge rather than continuous and preventive.

These problems are behavioural as much as technical. Digital systems only create value when administrators adopt them, leaders reinforce them, reviewers trust them, and donors understand them.

## 3. Proposed Solution

AGP will provide a three-part platform:

1. **AmanahHub:** public donor-facing charity directory with verified profiles, Amanah Index trust scores, and non-custodial donation positioning.
2. **amanahOS:** organization workspace for profiles, projects, reports, accounting, governance, compliance, policy kit, certification, trust score, and member management.
3. **AGP Console:** reviewer and evaluator control plane for trust events, governance cases, clarifications, scholar approval, publication control, compliance monitoring, and audit logs.

The platform is supported by an Amanah scoring engine and Supabase-backed evidence/audit records.

## 6. Objectives

1. Deploy a working digital governance platform with a JAKIM/MAIN pilot cohort.
2. Improve the completeness and timeliness of governance evidence and reporting.
3. Evaluate whether visible trust scores and verified profiles improve donor confidence.
4. Build a practical capacity-building model for mosque and NGO administrators.
5. Register the agreed authority-side organization cohort, including mosques, suraus, Islamic NGOs, waqf initiatives, and other approved entities.
6. Produce a replicable research-backed blueprint for scaling AGP across Malaysian Islamic social finance institutions.

## 7. Methodology

The project will use a mixed-method pilot evaluation:

- **Baseline assessment:** governance readiness, digital readiness, reporting practices, and trust perception.
- **Training intervention:** practical workshops on amanah, transparency, evidence management, reporting, and platform usage.
- **Institutional registration:** AGP registers the agreed list of organizations under JAKIM/MAIN authority, then activates accounts as organization members begin updating records over time.
- **Platform deployment:** participants use amanahOS while reviewers and institutional partners use AGP Console and public profiles appear on AmanahHub where appropriate.
- **Continuous analytics:** usage logs, evidence submissions, review cycles, remediation closure, and Amanah score movement.
- **Qualitative study:** semi-structured interviews with organization administrators, donors, reviewers, and scholars.
- **Endline assessment:** compare baseline and endline results to measure behaviour and capability change.

## 11. Sustainability

AGP's long-term sustainability model is deliberately structured so that charity organizations do not pay AGP. The platform is intended as public-interest infrastructure. Initial development, expansion, and operational readiness will be supported through grants. Governance onboarding programmes can be sponsored by corporate ESG/CSR partners. Long-term continuity will come from alignment with zakat and waqf ecosystems, where institutions benefit from stronger governance, audit-readiness, reporting consistency, and sector-wide trust data.

AGP may charge zakat bodies, waqf institutions, foundations, councils, CSR funders, or other institutional sponsors for service packages delivered to charity organizations. These sponsored service lines include:

- Organisation SaaS subscription.
- Managed bookkeeping service.
- Audit-readiness programme.
- Certification support.
- Institutional dashboard subscription.
- CSR/funder reporting tools.
- Training and governance workshops.
- Reviewer and Shariah marketplace.

This model keeps AGP accessible to charity organizations while creating a diversified support base through funders and institutions that have a direct interest in public trust.

## 14. Risk Management

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Low adoption by volunteer administrators | Hands-on onboarding, simplified workflows, sponsor-funded access, WhatsApp-style support rhythm, USM training design |
| Concern over public scoring | Begin with developmental framing, publish only reviewed outputs, allow clarifications and remediation |
| Data privacy concerns | PDPA-aligned minimization, role-based access, evidence privacy defaults, consent protocol |
| Institutional hesitation | Position as public-interest capability-building and trust improvement, not punitive surveillance or a new cost imposed on charities |
| MAIN/JAKIM data-list sensitivity | Begin with limited pilot scope, authority-approved fields, private-by-default evidence, and staged public publication |
| Technical delivery risk | Scope pilot around existing repo capabilities, prioritize stable onboarding/report/evidence/trust loops |

## 15. Expected Impact

By the end of the pilot, the project should demonstrate:

- stronger governance behaviour among participating organizations;
- clearer donor-facing trust information;
- faster review and clarification cycles;
- measurable organizational learning through remediation closure;

- a fundable scale narrative for Malaysian Islamic social finance governance;

AGP gives Malaysia a practical path to digitise amanah. With AGP as the direct applicant, JAKIM/MAIN as development partners, Prof. Anuar as advising member, and a diversified grant pipeline kept open, the project can move quickly while still proving that the platform changes governance behaviour in the institutions that hold community trust.
