# Amanah Platform Positioning Study

Date: 2026-05-20  
Decision question: Should Amanah continue, reposition, or retreat after seeing Hati.my, NGOBase, GlobalSadaqah, Kitafund, Seedkind, SimplyGiving, JomDonate, and other crowdfunding/directory platforms?

## Executive Decision

Do not retreat from Amanah. Retreat only from the idea of becoming "another crowdfunding platform."

The right position is:

> Amanah Governance Platform is Malaysia-first trust infrastructure for Islamic charities, mosques, waqf, zakat-adjacent NGOs, and donor-funded institutions. It verifies governance, evidence, reporting discipline, Shariah-sensitive fund controls, and public transparency. Donations may be supported, but fundraising is not the core moat.

The market already has discovery directories and fundraising engines. It does not yet have a credible, Malaysia-first, Islamic-social-finance governance layer that combines:

- evidence vault
- certification workflow
- reviewer/scholar/auditor roles
- public trust profile
- append-only trust history
- restricted fund accounting
- non-custodial donation logging
- regulator/CSR-ready transparency reports

That is the gap Amanah should occupy.

## Market Map

### 1. Directory platforms

Hati.my is a free bilingual directory for Malaysian charities and non-profits. It says it exists to solve the lack of centralized charity information, hosts over 1,000 entries, and directs users to contact or donate to organizations directly. Its donation button is for Hati.my's own operating society, not for listed organizations.

NGOBase is a broad global NGO directory. It lists NGOs/charities across countries and includes job, event, volunteer, funding agency, and grant discovery features. It is useful for search, but not Malaysia-specific trust assurance.

Implication: Amanah should not compete as a generic NGO directory. A directory can be a front door, but the value must be verified trust depth.

### 2. Crowdfunding and fundraising platforms

Kitafund positions itself as a crowdfunding platform for medical, humanitarian, education, charity, community, disaster, and emergency campaigns. It allows campaigners to create campaigns, charges operating fees, verifies campaigns before publication, asks for updates before disbursement, publishes audited platform financial reports, and claims over RM27 million raised, 350,000+ donors, and 4,000+ campaigns.

GlobalSadaqah positions itself as an award-winning CSR, zakat, and waqf management platform. It works with religious bodies, foundations, banks, corporates, and the public. It lists major corporate/strategic partners and says charity partners go through due diligence. Its 2021 impact report shows campaign-level fundraising outputs, such as RM470k+ raised for Malaysia COVID relief and RM1.1m+ for Ramadan campaigns.

Seedkind presents itself as an Islamic crowdfunding platform with anonymous donation, donation tracking, Shariah alignment, project review/screening, campaign updates, and monitoring/review.

SimplyGiving/SimplyGiv style platforms focus on online giving tools, recurring donations, donor engagement, campaign forms, event fundraising, and nonprofit fundraising infrastructure.

JomDonate is a cautionary case. Reporting around the Aliff Teega/Aisyah Hijanah controversy brought attention to a claimed 20% commission and questions around platform verification and legitimacy of causes.

Implication: Amanah cannot win by being a prettier donation page. The category is crowded, trust-fragile, and exposed to scandal risk. Donation rails should remain non-custodial and secondary.

### 3. Charity assurance platforms

Charity Navigator rates US public charities using domains such as Impact & Measurement, Accountability & Finance, Leadership & Planning, and Culture & Compensation. Its model shows donors value structured, comparable assurance.

Give.org / BBB Wise Giving Alliance uses a standards-based approach instead of ranking charities against each other. It reviews governance, finances, effectiveness reporting, transparency, and fundraising practices, then publishes reports.

GlobalGiving performs due diligence on partner organizations, reviewing local registration, legal documents, financial records, program materials, senior staff, and board information.

Implication: The global precedent supports Amanah's model. The missing local version is not only a rating website. Malaysia needs a live operating system for collecting evidence, maintaining fund controls, and producing trust outputs.

## Local Demand Signals

Malaysia has a large, fragmented social sector. A New Straits Times report citing the Federal Territories Registrar of Societies stated there were 95,694 registered NGOs in Malaysia excluding political NGOs as of May 2024. The same report highlighted risks of NGO misuse, including money laundering and terrorist financing, and emphasized proper registration and bank accounts in the society's name.

The Islamic giving market is large. Lembaga Zakat Selangor's 2024 annual report recorded RM1.22 billion in zakat collection and RM1.19 billion in distribution, with 380,000+ muzakki. LZS also reports Shariah compliance governance, trusteeship, and structured accountability. This proves that when governance is visible, public trust can sustain very large flows.

Policy direction is favorable. In July 2024, Malaysian reporting said the government was studying a Charities Act and Charities Commission to address misuse of donated funds. Even if legislation moves slowly, the direction is toward greater charity oversight.

Fraud and donor anxiety are real. Recent reporting and public discussion around donation misuse, commissions, social media fundraising, and platform verification show an opening for a system that makes evidence and fund discipline visible without taking custody of money.

## Amanah's Current Asset Base

The current repo already supports a stronger position than the market initially suggests. Based on the product docs and architecture map, Amanah has:

- AmanahHub public donor discovery and charity profile surfaces.
- AmanahOS/org workspace direction for governance, evidence, projects, reports, accounting, policy kit, and certification applications.
- AGP Console for reviewer, scholar, auditor, and platform governance workflows.
- CTCF certification model with gate criteria and weighted layers.
- Amanah Index rolling trust score based on governance, financial transparency, project transparency, impact efficiency, and feedback/trust signals.
- Append-only trust events and Amanah score history.
- Evidence privacy rules: private by default, public only with approval.
- Non-custodial donation principle: funds go direct to charity; Amanah logs and verifies events.
- Fund accounting direction for zakat, sadaqah, waqf, general, and project funds.

This is not just a fundraising idea. It is closer to "Candid/GuideStar + Charity Navigator + Islamic fund governance + org reporting workspace," localized for Malaysia.

## Competitive Positioning

### Position to avoid

"Amanah is a charity crowdfunding platform."

This is dangerous because:

- Kitafund, GlobalSadaqah, Seedkind, SimplyGiving, FundToGive, and others already occupy donation flow.
- Paid acquisition for donors is expensive.
- Campaign marketplaces reward emotional urgency, not governance discipline.
- Holding or disbursing funds creates legal, operational, reputational, and compliance risk.
- Donation scandals can contaminate the whole category.

### Position to own

"Amanah is a trust, governance, and transparency infrastructure layer for Islamic social finance organizations."

This is stronger because:

- It solves a harder, less crowded problem.
- It can partner with crowdfunding platforms instead of fighting them.
- It can sell to organizations, councils, CSR teams, grant makers, universities, auditors, and foundations.
- It aligns with future charity regulation.
- It creates defensible data and workflows, not just campaign pages.

### One-line market category

Malaysia-first Islamic charity governance and trust infrastructure.

### One-line donor message

Give with more confidence: see verified governance, evidence, fund discipline, and reporting history before you support a cause.

### One-line organization message

Turn your governance, reports, evidence, and fund controls into a public trust profile that donors, CSR partners, and reviewers can understand.

### One-line regulator/CSR message

Monitor charity readiness, evidence completeness, restricted fund discipline, and transparency progress across a cohort without relying on manual spreadsheets.

## Strategic Recommendation

Continue, but reposition immediately.

The winning strategy is a wedge strategy:

1. Start with 10 to 20 organizations that already care about trust: mosques, tahfiz schools, Islamic NGOs, waqf-related bodies, zakat-adjacent charity programs, and CSR-funded community organizations.
2. Offer Amanah as a governance readiness and transparency workspace, not as a donor marketplace.
3. Produce public trust profiles and private improvement reports.
4. Invite a credible partner, preferably university, SIRC/MAIN-adjacent advisor, audit/accounting partner, or Islamic finance body, to validate the CTCF and Amanah Index.
5. Use donation features only after trust profiles and reporting workflows are credible.

## Product Strategy

### Keep

- Public charity profiles.
- Amanah Index and CTCF.
- Reviewer/scholar workflow.
- Evidence upload and public approval.
- Fund accounting and compliance reporting.
- Non-custodial donation logging.

### De-emphasize

- Generic crowdfunding.
- "Raise money fast" language.
- Emotional campaign marketplace UI.
- Any feature that requires Amanah to custody, pool, or decide disbursement of funds.

### Add or sharpen

- Public trust profile pages that are visibly different from campaign pages.
- "Verified evidence checklist" per organization.
- "Governance improvement plan" for each org.
- Exportable CSR/regulator due diligence pack.
- Clear disclaimers: Amanah verifies evidence and governance discipline; it does not guarantee spiritual merit, future performance, or absence of all misconduct.
- Integration path for external donation platforms: "Use Kitafund/GlobalSadaqah/your bank QR for collection; use Amanah to prove governance and report impact."

## Business Model

Recommended revenue stack:

| Customer | Offer | Pricing direction |
|---|---|---|
| Small mosque/NGO | Basic Amanah profile + evidence checklist | Free or sponsored |
| Growing NGO/tahfiz/mosque | AmanahOS governance workspace + public trust profile | RM50-RM200/month or RM500-RM2,000/year |
| CSR/foundation donor | Cohort due diligence dashboard + export reports | Annual license or project fee |
| SIRC/MAIN/local authority | Oversight dashboard and reporting pack | Pilot grant / enterprise license |
| Auditor/accounting partner | Review workflow and evidence pack | Service partnership |
| Fundraising platforms | Amanah badge/API or trust report integration | B2B/API fee later |

Avoid relying mainly on transaction fees. Transaction fees pull Amanah back into the crowded donation-platform fight and may damage the neutrality of trust scoring.

## Go-To-Market Wedge

### Best first beachhead

Islamic NGOs and mosque-linked community funds that already receive public donations but lack clean governance reporting.

Why:

- Pain is obvious: donors ask for proof, committees use manual records, reports are fragmented.
- They can benefit even before donor marketplace liquidity exists.
- Amanah's Islamic fund/accountability language fits naturally.
- A small verified cohort can become proof for funders.

### Best first institutional partner

A university or research partner plus an Islamic governance/audit advisor.

Why:

- A regulator/SIRC partnership is ideal but slow.
- A university partner can validate methodology, publish pilot findings, and create legitimacy.
- An audit/accounting partner can turn evidence requirements into practical checklists.

### Pilot offer

"Amanah Trust Readiness Pilot"

Deliverables for each organization:

- baseline governance assessment
- evidence completeness score
- restricted fund readiness check
- public trust profile
- 90-day improvement plan
- board/committee summary report
- optional donation QR/payment link display, direct to organization

Success metrics:

- 80% of pilot orgs complete legal/governance evidence
- 70% publish at least one public-safe project/report evidence item
- 50% improve one trust band or resolve one material evidence gap
- 100% have organization-name bank account / clear payment destination displayed
- one cohort report suitable for CSR, grant, or SIRC conversation

## Retreat Criteria

Amanah should retreat or pause only if several of these happen:

- No credible partner will validate or co-own the trust methodology after 90 to 120 days of outreach.
- Fewer than 5 organizations agree to upload real governance/evidence documents in a pilot.
- The only customer demand is for fundraising pages, not governance/reporting.
- Legal advice says public scoring creates unacceptable defamation or regulatory exposure unless heavily redesigned.
- The team cannot support evidence review operations, even for a small cohort.

If these occur, do not shut everything down immediately. Narrow the product into a lightweight internal governance checklist/report generator for NGOs and rebuild later.

## 90-Day Action Plan

### Days 1-15: reposition

- Update pitch language from "crowdfunding platform" to "trust and governance infrastructure."
- Remove any first-slide comparison that makes Amanah look like Kitafund or GlobalSadaqah.
- Create three demo public profiles: strong, improving, insufficient evidence.
- Add trust-score disclaimers and methodology summary.

### Days 16-30: pilot recruitment

- Recruit 10 organizations with real governance pain.
- Ask for a letter of participation, not just verbal interest.
- Recruit one university/audit/Islamic finance advisor.
- Prepare a "Trust Readiness Checklist" as the first onboarding artifact.

### Days 31-60: operational proof

- Run evidence upload, review, and public profile publication.
- Produce the first cohort dashboard.
- Track reviewer time per organization.
- Identify which evidence requirements are too hard for small organizations.

### Days 61-90: funder proof

- Publish an anonymized pilot learning report.
- Prepare CSR/SIRC/foundation pitch with real before/after examples.
- Decide whether donation features remain secondary or become a supported add-on.
- Price the first paid governance workspace plan.

## Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Defamation / reputational risk | Public scores may be challenged | Use "evidence completeness" and "trust discipline" language, appeals process, disclaimers, versioned methodology |
| Reviewer bottleneck | Trust systems require human validation | Start with small cohort; use checklist automation; train certified reviewers later |
| Org resistance | Small orgs may fear exposure | Frame as improvement pathway, not punishment; allow private readiness mode before publication |
| Regulator complexity | SIRC/MAIN/JAKIM engagement can be slow | Start with university/audit partner; build evidence before formal regulator ask |
| Crowdfunding distraction | Donation features can consume all attention | Keep non-custodial donation display simple; focus on verified reporting |
| Methodology credibility | Proprietary score needs legitimacy | Publish v1 methodology, advisory board, pilot study, change log |

## Final Call

Proceed with Amanah, but make a deliberate category shift:

- From: Islamic charity crowdfunding and directory.
- To: Islamic charity governance, certification, and verified trust infrastructure.

The market evidence favors continuation. The user need is not "one more place to donate." It is "who can I trust, why, and what evidence proves it?" Amanah is already architected around that harder question. That is the lane worth defending.
