# AGP Product Presentation Diagram Plan

**Purpose:** Determine the visual proof objects that drive the Amanah Governance Platform (AGP) product presentation.

**Updated presentation thesis:** AGP provides governance infrastructure for charity organisations, including mosques, so trusted governance becomes part of daily organisational behaviour. Public trust, donation confidence, and authority monitoring are important outcomes of that infrastructure, not the starting point of the product story.

**Primary product audience:** charity organisations, mosques, waqf bodies, NGO operators, and their committees or management teams.

**Secondary public audience:** donors, CSR teams, grant makers, and communities who need clear, approved information before supporting an organisation through the organisation's own bank account, QR code, or donation link.

**Authority audience:** governing and oversight bodies, including Majlis Agama Islam Negeri for mosques and Islamic charity governance, that need a structured way to monitor organisations under their jurisdiction.

**Narrative rule:** Present AGP as organisational governance infrastructure first. It is not primarily a donation marketplace, crowdfunding platform, or public directory. The public trust layer works because the organisation is already managing governance, evidence, funds, and reporting through AGP.

## The Story Arc

| Act | Audience question | Diagram that answers it | Intended conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Organisational behaviour | How does a charity or mosque make trusted governance part of daily work? | Governance infrastructure map | AGP gives the organisation a structured operating system for records, controls, evidence, funds, projects, and reporting. |
| 2. Product system | What does AGP connect inside and around the organisation? | Three-surface platform map | amanahOS, AGP Console, and AmanahHub serve different users while sharing one governed trust record. |
| 3. Governance workflow | How is governance evidence prepared, reviewed, and improved? | Governance workflow | Trust is created through routine evidence capture, review, improvement actions, and approved outputs. |
| 4. Fund accountability | How are restricted Islamic/social funds governed? | Restricted-fund accountability flow | Zakat, sadaqah, waqf, general, and project funds can retain their purpose through records, evidence, review, and reporting. |
| 5. Authority oversight | How can a governing body monitor organisations under its jurisdiction? | Authority oversight dashboard map | Authorities can see cohort readiness, compliance status, evidence gaps, and review progress without manually chasing fragmented reports. |
| 6. Public confidence | What does the public gain from the governance infrastructure? | Public trust and donation layer | Donors and CSR partners receive approved trust information and can support through the organisation's own donation channels. |
| 7. Category position | What category does AGP own? | Positioning map | AGP is the governance and trust infrastructure layer around charitable operations and public giving. |
| 8. Stakeholder value | Why does this matter to the ecosystem? | Stakeholder value loop | Better organisational governance creates better oversight, better public confidence, and stronger institutional readiness. |

## Diagram Set

### 1. Organisational Governance Infrastructure: Trusted Governance as Daily Behaviour

**Slide claim:** AGP helps charity organisations and mosques operate with trusted governance every day, not only when preparing annual reports or fundraising campaigns.

**Job in the story:** Establish the core product purpose before discussing donors or the public. This is now the opening diagram.

**Visual structure:** A central organisation operating surface with six daily governance areas feeding one governed organisational record.

```text
Charity organisation / mosque committee
        |
        v
AGP governance infrastructure
        |
        +-- Governance records
        +-- Committee decisions and approvals
        +-- Fund accounts and restricted purposes
        +-- Project / activity evidence
        +-- Policies, documents, and audit trail
        +-- Reports and improvement actions
        |
        v
Governed organisational record
```

**Key modules to show:** `governance records`, `fund discipline`, `evidence vault`, `committee approvals`, `project/activity records`, `reporting`.

**What the diagram proves:** AGP is infrastructure for organisational conduct. Public trust is credible because governance records are maintained at the source.

**Design direction:** Use the organisation or mosque as the hero, with AGP surrounding it as operational infrastructure. The tone should feel institutional, trusted, and practical.

### 2. Three-Surface Product Map: One Trust System, Three Experiences

**Slide claim:** AGP connects organisational governance, oversight review, and approved public transparency through one shared trust system.

**Visual structure:** Three vertical lanes feeding a central governed trust record.

| Surface | Primary user | Core actions | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| `amanahOS` | Charity organisation, mosque committee, NGO staff | Maintain governance records, fund accounts, project/activity evidence, approvals, policies, and reports | Daily governance discipline and submission-ready evidence |
| `AGP Console` | Reviewer, scholar, auditor, platform authority, Majlis Agama Islam Negeri or other governing body | Review evidence, monitor organisations, assess readiness, record decisions, and request improvements | Governance oversight, compliance visibility, and verified decisions |
| `AmanahHub` | Donor, CSR partner, grant maker, community member | Discover organisations, understand approved trust information, and donate through organisation-owned channels | Better-informed support decision |

**Central engine labels:** `governed trust record`, `CTCF certification`, `Amanah Index`, `trust events`, `evidence permissions`, `audit trail`, `report generation`.

**Connector meaning:**

- Solid arrow from organisation to engine: source record, evidence, approval, or report.
- Solid arrow from console to engine: review, decision, monitoring, or improvement request.
- Dashed arrow to public surface: approved public information only.

**Design direction:** Do not make the public layer visually bigger than the organisation layer. The product story begins with organisational governance and extends outward.

### 3. Governance Workflow: From Daily Records to Governed Trust

**Slide claim:** AGP turns routine organisational activity into reviewable governance evidence.

**Visual structure:** A six-stage sequence with an explicit improvement loop.

```text
1. Organisation records daily governance
   decisions, funds, activities, projects, documents
             |
2. Evidence vault
   private by default; classified by purpose and access
             |
3. Internal approval
   committee / authorised officer confirms records
             |
4. Independent or authority review
   reviewer / scholar / auditor / governing body checks evidence
             |
5. Improvement and decision
   clarification, corrective action, certification, trust event
             |
6. Approved outputs
   authority view, transparency report, public trust profile
```

**Critical annotation:** `Public disclosure is an approved output, not automatic publication.`

**What the diagram proves:** AGP supports continuous governance behaviour and a controlled review process. A trust badge or public profile is only the visible result.

**Presenter cue:** Spend more time on stages 1 to 4. That is where AGP changes organisational behaviour.

### 4. Restricted-Fund Accountability: Every Ringgit Keeps Its Purpose

**Slide claim:** AGP helps organisations govern restricted funds by preserving purpose, evidence, approvals, and reporting.

**Visual structure:** A swimlane flow by fund type. Use one lane each for `Zakat`, `Sadaqah`, `Waqf`, `General`, and `Project-restricted` funds.

```text
Receipt / commitment
        -> assigned fund purpose
        -> recorded transaction and supporting evidence
        -> internal approval and restricted-use check
        -> activity / beneficiary / project linkage
        -> balance, report, and review trail
```

**Control points to mark:** `purpose classification`, `restricted-use check`, `supporting evidence`, `approval trail`, `reviewable balance`, `reportable output`.

**What the diagram proves:** Financial accountability is not merely an annual PDF. It is a continuing control trail that respects fund purpose.

**Scope note:** Present this as the AGP accounting and reporting direction unless live product proof is available for every fund type shown. Do not imply that AGP holds, pools, or disburses donor money.

### 5. Authority Oversight: Monitoring Organisations Under Jurisdiction

**Slide claim:** AGP gives governing bodies a structured view of the organisations they oversee, including mosques under Majlis Agama Islam Negeri.

**Job in the story:** Show the third-party institutional value of AGP. This is especially important for Islamic charity governance and mosque oversight.

**Visual structure:** A cohort dashboard map.

```text
Mosques / charities under jurisdiction
        -> governance submissions and evidence packs
        -> AGP Console review and monitoring
        -> authority oversight view
        -> improvement actions, certification status, cohort reporting
```

**Authority view zones:**

| Zone | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Organisation registry | mosques, charities, registration status, responsible officers |
| Governance readiness | records complete, policies present, committee approvals, missing evidence |
| Fund discipline | fund purpose separation, reporting gaps, review flags |
| Review pipeline | submitted, clarification requested, reviewed, approved, escalated |
| Cohort reporting | district/state view, trends, risk areas, improvement progress |

**What the diagram proves:** AGP can reduce manual monitoring burden and create comparable governance visibility across a jurisdiction.

**Design direction:** Use a governing body at the top, a cohort of organisations below, and a monitoring console on the side. Avoid making it look like surveillance; frame it as governance support and improvement.

### 6. Public Trust and Donation Layer: Approved Information Before Support

**Slide claim:** The public can support charities with better confidence because AGP turns internal governance into approved public information.

**Job in the story:** Reframe the trust gap as a public-facing benefit of AGP, not the primary reason AGP exists.

**Visual structure:** A public profile flow.

```text
Governed organisational record
        -> approved public trust profile
        -> transparency report and evidence timeline
        -> direct support through organisation-owned bank / QR / donation link
```

**Public information zones:**

| Zone | Public information shown |
|---|---|
| Identity and status | organisation identity, verification / certification state, methodology link |
| Trust summary | Amanah Index, pillar signals, last reviewed date |
| Evidence timeline | approved milestones, reports, certification history, trust events |
| Fund and project transparency | public-safe fund reporting and project/activity updates |
| Support action | direct organisation bank / QR / approved external donation link |

**Privacy boundary:** A visible lock or divider must distinguish `approved public evidence` from the private evidence vault.

**Non-custodial wording:** `AGP enables trusted discovery and support. The organisation receives donations through its own channels. AGP does not custody, pool, or disburse donor money.`

### 7. Category Positioning: AGP Is the Governance and Trust Infrastructure Layer

**Slide claim:** AGP sits around charitable operations, oversight, and public confidence; it does not replace donation rails or authority mandates.

**Visual structure:** Four horizontal layers.

```text
Public confidence layer       Donors | CSR teams | Grant makers | Communities
                              ------------------------------------------------
Governance authority layer    Majlis Agama Islam Negeri | regulators | auditors
                              ------------------------------------------------
AGP infrastructure layer      Evidence | Review | Certification | Fund discipline | Reports
                              ------------------------------------------------
Operating / payment layer     NGOs | Mosques | Waqf bodies | Bank QR | Donation platforms
```

**Key labels:** `Governance-first`, `Non-custodial`, `Evidence-based`, `Private by default`, `Public when approved`, `Authority-ready`, `Append-only trust history`.

**What the diagram proves:** AGP can integrate with existing bank QR codes, fundraising platforms, and governing structures while owning the governance and trust infrastructure category.

**Design direction:** Make the AGP infrastructure layer the strongest visual band. Payment rails should be present but visually secondary.

### 8. Trust Engine: How AGP Makes Governance Legible

**Slide claim:** AGP converts organisational governance signals into a transparent, traceable trust view.

**Visual structure:** Five input pillars arranged around a central score, certification, and history object.

| Input pillar | Example evidence or signal |
|---|---|
| Governance | registration, committee information, policies, approvals |
| Financial transparency | statements, reconciliations, restricted-fund records |
| Project/activity transparency | plans, progress reports, approved public evidence |
| Impact discipline | outcome reporting, delivery evidence, beneficiary or community context |
| Review and trust signals | verified reviews, authority decisions, resolved evidence gaps, historical events |

**Central object:** `Amanah Index + CTCF status + append-only trust history`.

**Output rail:** `Organisation improvement plan`, `authority cohort view`, `public trust profile`, `transparency report`.

**Safety language on the slide:** `A trust indicator is evidence-based and versioned; it is not a guarantee of future performance or spiritual merit.`

**Design direction:** Avoid a single opaque dial. Use a pillar composition with a small history line below it, so the audience sees both current state and progression over time.

### 9. Stakeholder Value Loop: Governance Creates Confidence

**Slide claim:** Better organisational governance creates better oversight, public confidence, and institutional readiness.

**Visual structure:** A clockwise loop with five actors.

```text
Organisation improves records and controls
        -> Governing body monitors and guides improvement
        -> Reviewer validates evidence and decisions
        -> Public / CSR partner gains confidence to engage
        -> Support, partnerships, and expectations strengthen the organisation
```

**Outcome labels:**

- Organisation: lower reporting friction, clearer improvement path, stronger governance readiness.
- Mosque / charity committee: better internal discipline, continuity, and evidence for decisions.
- Governing authority: consistent evidence packs, cohort-level oversight, targeted improvement.
- CSR / grant maker: faster due diligence and more comparable partner choices.
- Donor / community: confidence grounded in visible governance and progress.

**Design direction:** This must be a true loop, not boxes with decorative arrows. Each arrow needs a concrete exchange: evidence, monitoring, validation, confidence, or support.

## Minimum Viable Set

If the presentation must stay concise, build these five diagrams first:

1. Organisational Governance Infrastructure
2. Three-Surface Product Map
3. Governance Workflow
4. Restricted-Fund Accountability
5. Authority Oversight

The public trust and donation layer should be added after the audience understands that AGP starts with organisational governance. Category positioning and stakeholder value are best added for partnership, regulator, grant, or investor audiences.

## Visual System Rules

- Use one diagram language throughout: warm paper or light stone base, deep green/ink for governed systems, gold accent only for verified or approved outputs, muted red only for gaps or risks.
- Show organisations and mosques as active governance operators, not passive recipients of public donations.
- Use real labels: `governance records`, `evidence vault`, `CTCF certification`, `Amanah Index`, `restricted-fund record`, `authority oversight`, and `approved public evidence`.
- Use arrows only for movement, decision, or causality. A line without a clear meaning should be removed.
- Separate private, authority, and public information in every relevant diagram.
- Make non-custodial design explicit in Diagram 6 and Diagram 7.
- Do not use invented scores, pilot outcomes, client logos, regulator endorsements, or product screenshots. Use labelled illustrative states until validated product proof is available.

## Evidence and Claims Checklist

Before final slide production, confirm the following:

- Which modules are live, pilot-ready, or roadmap-only.
- The approved wording for the Amanah Index and CTCF methodology disclaimer.
- The fund types and accounting controls that can be demonstrated in a real environment.
- Which authority workflows can be positioned for Majlis Agama Islam Negeri or other governing bodies.
- Any verified institutional partner, pilot organisation, mosque, charity, or implementation result that may be named.
- Whether the deck is tailored primarily for an `organisation / mosque`, `governing authority`, `CSR / foundation`, `regulator`, or `grant / investor` audience.
